02State ownership

One wheel-spin integrator exists in the whole system

CarMaker’s went out with the PowerTrain module. The hardware’s physical spin is servo-slaved. Ours is the only one — which is why nothing can diverge.

03Signal flow

What crosses the cut at step k

The actual dyno shaft speed never reaches CarMaker. It is seen only by the dyno’s internal speed-tracking controller.

04Timing · the key diagram

Where the one-step delay actually lives

The tire’s slip→force map is memoryless. The delay is not in the tire — it is in the shared variable, because Tire is scheduled before PowerTrain and Rim_rotv is written only once per cycle.

05Two integrations

The wheel integration is not after PowerTrain — it is PowerTrain

These are routinely conflated. They happen at different points in the sequence and are owned by different parties.

A · ours

Wheel spin wi

Happens at the PowerTrain slot. CM natively does it in the DriveLine — “Integration of rotation speeds”, RefMan Fig. 16.83. In our build, our Simulink block is this step.

B · CarMaker

Body & suspension DOFs

Happens at Body Frame, after PowerTrain. This is the one that follows us. Figure 1.23 brackets the whole vehicle-model evaluation as “One integration step”.

Tire reads Rim_rotv = w[k−1], emits Trq_T2W[k] (memoryless map) Brake PowerTrain INTEGRATION A: w[k] = w[k−1] + (Ts/I)(Taxl[k] + Trq_T2W[k]) Body Frame INTEGRATION B: advances chassis / suspension states

CM’s solver is Backward Euler

Programmer’s Guide, verbatim: “Since the solver used in CarMaker is backwards-euler, the user needs to choose this option. Due to the better compatibility, vibrations are no longer an issue.” Our K·Ts·z/(z−1) matches CM’s own solver — not just IPG’s reference model.

the one rule

Add no further delay

The module ordering supplies exactly one step, free. Any Memory, Unit Delay or rate transition on the path Trq_T2W → integrator → rotv makes d = 2, costing a further Δζ ≈ 0.14.

08Stability

A ~45 Hz mode whose damping grows with speed

Wheel inertia against tire relaxation gives a second-order system. Its natural frequency is speed-independent; its damping is proportional to speed. The one-step delay subtracts a fixed amount.

09Verdict

Why it works, and the two things it cannot tell you

why it works

One integrator

Model/plant divergence needs two states drifting apart. There is one. CM’s tire slip is computed from exactly the number that commands the dyno.

why it works

IPG’s sanctioned bypass

Backward Euler at 1 ms with Wheel.<i>.I read from CM parameters — block-for-block identical to UserPowerTrain.mdl, and matching CM’s own solver.

why it works

Bounded, not divergent

A 2 N·m torque bias yields 1×10−4 slip offset and ~0.24 m/s speed offset — the tire supplies restoring slip stiffness, the body supplies road-load drag.

blind spot

The servo error is invisible

CM never sees w_act. If the servo saturates, we integrate a torque measured at the wrong operating point. Nothing rings, nothing diverges — the sim quietly becomes a different vehicle. Log w_act − w_cmd and alarm on it.

blind spot

Taxl cannot be validated against CM

CarMaker never sees Taxl dynamically, so a torque-measurement error surfaces only as that ~0.24 m/s bias — indistinguishable from a dozen modelling choices. Calibration must be established on the bench.

Still open

Model.USE_MODE in the tire file — it decides both whether combined slip exists (paths A/C) and which stability analysis applies. Expected 13 / 14 / 15. Also Cs, for the 45 Hz figure.

Recorded simplifications

All CM brake torque zeroed (real braking arrives inside Taxl). Trq_Supp2WC, Trq_Supp2Bdy1, Trq_Supp2BdyEng zeroed — no drive-torque squat or engine-mount reaction.
